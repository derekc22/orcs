The accepted box is already still when descent begins. Its last stationary second has angular-speed RMS 0.00410 rad/s and angular-acceleration RMS 0.10691 rad/s². Residual walking/braking motion is not the dominant cause. The accepted 4 s support transition is therefore preserved exactly.

The regression is generated during descent: 92.9% of placement angular-acceleration energy precedes initial terrain contact. Before fork opening, object acceleration RMS is 33.17 rad/s² with hand-force standard deviations 0.73/0.98 N. Between fork opening and touchdown those values rise to 48.06 rad/s² and 1.20/1.13 N. Root sway contributes in the lower crouch, while palm-relative object motion confirms that the grasp is compliant rather than rigidly following the body. Object torque fluctuations come from actual hand contacts while terrain force is zero. These are not disturbances generated solely at release.

Early descent acceleration has a strong 49–50 Hz component matching the existing arm IK solve period. That controller holds each new arm position target for four physics steps. Interpolating the arm position reference across those four 5 ms steps, with the original grip law unchanged, reduces first-second acceleration RMS from 44.40 to 14.14 rad/s². This controlled ablation supports sampled arm-reference excitation of the compliant hand/box system. It does not establish that every later oscillation has the same origin.

The original carriage control is `q + (preload + 3*v)/800`, while the unchanged physical actuator contributes `-800*q - 3*v`. Away from clipping, the actual net carriage force is therefore `preload`: native damping is canceled. The refinement commands net damping of 6 N·s/m through the existing finite actuator, plus 0.7 N·m·s/rad arm damping through the existing arm actuators. No model damping, gains, contact properties, mass, force cap or morphology is edited. Together with reference interpolation, this reduces precontact carriage-speed RMS from 0.141/0.167 to 0.0313/0.0266 m/s, first-second 45–55 Hz acceleration power by 89.3%, and slower box/root motion. Arm and carriage damping were tested together; their individual causal contributions are not separately identified.

Terrain contact is secondary but still matters. The accepted touchdown has 0.882 rad/s angular speed, 0.172 m/s downward COM speed and 7.82° tilt from its initial terrain-supported up axis. The selected descent reaches contact at 0.328 rad/s, 0.0475 m/s downward and 6.06°. Thus it enters load transfer with less rotational and downward momentum. Asymmetric contact and compliant rocking remain measurable; a perfectly flat or rigid grasp is not claimed.

Integration found an additional arm-reference discontinuity at the terrain-load confirmation boundary. Resetting the IK seed to the actual joint positions bypassed the interpolated reference and abruptly unloaded terrain on the first opening step. Removing this controller-memory reset and rechecking current physical support fixes that boundary. Paired runs have identical controls, qpos, qvel and contacts through all 8,028 controls before opening, and exactly the same opening time. Opening-step terrain load rises from 0.260 to 0.589 N against 0.4905 N box weight. This is a physical controller correction, not a changed metric window or delayed frame alignment.

Four focused placement continuations are preserved. Interpolation alone is rejected for a 0.001805 N·s left-toe terrain strike. Damping alone passes numerical physical checks but leaves much more acceleration. Interpolation plus damping with the old seed reset is rejected for the opening-load gate. The selected version adds continuity at that same handoff. The lowering plan, physical fork/opening/withdrawal trajectories and sustained support gates remain the accepted ones. Walking, pickup and the stationary support transition are unchanged. No parameter sweep or further search was conducted.
